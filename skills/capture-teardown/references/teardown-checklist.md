# Capture teardown checklist

## Per screen

Record these in the screen's own words. A characterisation is not reusable; a
quote is.

- **Route or filename**, so the next reader can reopen the exact capture.
- **The literal headline**, verbatim, including punctuation and register.
- **What is asked for**, field by field. A form with four fields is four asks.
- **What the primary control says**, and — separately — **which control is styled
 as primary**. These come apart deliberately: Lindy styles *"Continue without
 connecting"* as the black button, so the confident-looking control is the one
 that skips.
- **The escape hatch's exact wording and prominence.** "Skip for now" in grey and
 "Continue without tools" in a modal are different beats of the same idea.
- **What is conspicuously absent.** Vellum's reading screen has no progress bar,
 no spinner, and no list — one centred line on an empty ground. The absence is
 the design.
- **Whether any claim on screen carries a source.** Most cannot.

## Per flow

- Number the screens in order and name each beat.
- Note where a beat is **split** that could have been merged. Vellum's reading and
 findings are two screens, and the split is the whole trick: one held and empty,
 the next dense and correctable.
- Note what the flow **ends at**. Twelve screens ending at an empty chat box is a
 different product than four ending at a card form.
- Note what is **persistent across screens** and whether it accumulates.
- Note where the flow asks for something it could derive, and where it derives
 something it could have asked for — and why. Vellum asks for a name before
 researching because it needs a seed; that is a reason, not an oversight.

## Against existing claims

For every spec row, decision page, or annotation that cites these captures:

- Does the cited capture actually show the cited thing?
- Is it at the **flow position** the citation implies?
- Is the behaviour **as strong** as claimed?
- Is the pattern attributed to the right source, or do both competitors do it?

Each mismatch is a finding.

## Disagreements this method has already caught

From a 2026-08-29 review of a capture folder, against the product's own
onboarding spec, §7:

| Claim | What the screen shows |
|---|---|
| `v13` = "auto-configure from what was learned", cited as the model for step 3 | `v13` is the **closing** screen. What it auto-configures is *capability* — "I've set myself up with plugins around who you are", then two named plugin cards |
| `v19` = "first message references the research", cited as proof the context took | The first message names three topics the research supplied and **cites none of them**. It gestures; it cannot point, because the research was scraped |
| "Persistent accumulating visualization — Lindy, all 4 steps" | True, and it also points at `v20`, Vellum's identity page — which is not a step but where the flow **lands**, printing "0 memories" and "nothing scheduled yet" without flinching |
| Escape hatch attributed to Lindy | **Both** do it. Vellum's "Continue without account" is on its very first screen |

Two further findings the summary had no row for at all:

- **Lindy's `04` marketing section**: *"Its memory is yours to edit — it all lives
 in plain files. Open the memory folder, read what it remembers, and change it
 like a doc."* A competitor is already selling the editable-plain-file brain.
- **Permission asks have a good and a bad shape in the same folder.** Roam (`r1`)
 asks for three permissions with a sentence of reason and an Allow on each.
 Instinct (`instinct/IMG_7212–13`) sends the user to eleven Google scopes with
 one "Select all" and no reason anywhere.

## The failure this exists to prevent

An FTUE was designed on 2026-08-29 from the spec's one-line rows. The
wiki already held the detail — `sources/2026-08-16-lindy-onboarding-teardown`,
recorded thirteen days earlier — and an existing concepts page on the same
pattern existed
with the stated purpose *"so the next session starts here instead of re-deriving
them."* Neither was opened.

The rebuild that followed claimed in its own canvas annotation to have been "rebuilt
after looking at the actual screens", having looked at four of forty-nine. It
carried every drift the terminal row had been corrected for a day earlier.

**A spec that restates a wiki page competes with it, and the restatement wins,
because it sits closer to the work.** Cite; do not paraphrase.
